What really stabilizes diffusion RL?

Two findings change how we build the training objective.

A hidden force survives cancellation.

NFT-style training does more than follow rewards. Generated endpoints define an ideal forward velocity that can differ from the model's reverse-sampling velocity. This gap creates an extra consistency force.

FNFT=2g+(2q−1)e
Reward guidanceHidden consistency force

Positive and negative branches oppose this force, but centering rewards does not guarantee cancellation at each state.

What do the symbols mean?

This is the normalized output-space force at the on-policy initialization. The goodness weight r lies in [0,1]; q is its conditional mean at the state, and g = Cov(r, uₜ | sₜ), where uₜ is the forward velocity target. The consistency gap e is the old policy's ideal forward velocity minus its reverse-sampling velocity. See Section 3.1 of the paper.

EMA helps through restoration,
not off-policy data alone.

Keeping EMA-generated data but removing its restoring reference still leads to collapse in our ablation. The reference anchor is the crucial difference.

Small in output space does not mean small after aggregation: restoration has a smaller output-force RMS but a larger parameter-gradient norm than guidance.

9.4× smaller output-force RMS.
4.9× larger parameter-gradient norm.

Restoration versus guidance in the NFT ablation, averaged over the second updates of rollouts 0–17. These are observed ratios, not a universal scaling law.

Change the order. Remove the force.

Aggregate endpoints and rewards before regression, then reuse a frozen target.

One direction for each prompt

Reward–endpoint covariance supplies shared guidance on the states actually visited by the reverse sampler. Normalize after aggregation, rather than relying on samplewise forces to cancel.

G(c)=Cov¯(R,x0∣c)RMS(Cov¯)

For nonzero covariance; otherwise G(c) = 0.

R is reward, x₀ a clean latent endpoint, and c the prompt. The covariance is estimated over its K samples.

Two updates. One frozen target.

Fit vold + G on two disjoint minibatches. The first update follows guidance; the second also restores the model in proportion to its output drift.

First updateG
Second updateG−d

Directions in output space, up to the MSE scale and state weight; d = vθ − vold. A trust-region mask suppresses excessive outward updates.

No differentiable reward model.
No EMA schedule to tune.

Measurement protocol and data

All methods use an eight-update causal EMA for crossing measurement. Targets are unsmoothed baseline means; crossings use observed points, without interpolation. Elapsed GPU hours include evaluation and waits. The baseline levels are empirical late-stage rewards, not a guarantee of asymptotic convergence.
